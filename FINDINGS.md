# Findings in detail

What broke, in order, across the 30 runs on the local bench (one machine, local Next.js and Supabase stack; not a hosted figure). Every sentence below is generated by `npm run report` from `results/runs/`. Numbers in square brackets refer to the sources at the end of the [README](README.md#sources-read-2026-10-11). Short version: [REPORT.md](REPORT.md).

1. **Auth at arrival, if arrival creates or signs in users behind one address.** With the per-IP limiter switched on at the CLI's default limits, 200 arrivals over 120 s from one venue address: 30/200 new anonymous users, 41/200 new email sign-ups, 89/200 password logins and 89/200 refreshes got through; the rest received HTTP 429. Reusing an existing session: 200/200. With one forwarded address per device, the 2 paths tried (new anonymous user and password login) got 200/200 and 200/200. The first 429 for logins arrived after 35 s, once the burst of 30 was spent. Measured on the local Auth container with the limiter configured explicitly (it is off in the CLI stack by default: 40 anonymous sign-ins from one machine all succeeded).
2. **Realtime connection quota, at arrival.** The session needs 204 Realtime connections (200 devices, a facilitator, a display and 2 real browsers). With the tenant limits as seeded (200 concurrent connections, the published Free value), 4 protocol clients were refused in each of 3 runs (`ConnectionRateLimitReached: Too many connected users` x192 in the Realtime log, the clients retrying) and never saw a round result. Everything else stayed well inside the targets: decision p95 17 ms (17 ms to 19 ms), 0 acknowledged decisions lost. Changing only the tenant limits (`conference-raised`, 3 runs, same 2 browsers): 202/202 ready and 202/202 screens on every round's final result within 5 s.
3. **After a silent cut, the facilitator was blocked.** The venue link went silent for 20 s as round 1 closed (old connections left open, as when an access point loses its state), then came back. What the rehearsal found first is that the session itself broke. Across the 6 cut runs (3 with the client library's 25 s heartbeat, 3 with 5 s): the facilitator could not open round 3 in 4 (`upstream_unreachable`), and in 1 more its open request timed out although the round did open; the round 2 close failed in 3 and took 26 s in 2 more; in 5 of 6 runs, 24 to 40 of 120 decisions were never acknowledged. In 4 of those runs, another 40 decisions were rejected because round 3 had never opened. No acknowledged decision was lost. The facilitator is on the wired path, not the cut link, but its requests go through the same local API gateway as the reconnecting phones: that gateway (Kong in the CLI stack: 1 worker, 512 connections) logged `worker_connections are not enough` 142 to 806 times per run along with HTTP 500/503, while the old connections still held its slots. That is a ceiling of the CLI's local gateway, not a hosted Supabase figure; the question it raises for any deployment is what happens to the facilitator's actions while the room reconnects. The phones themselves: within 30 s of the link returning, 62 to 97 of 200 venue clients held the current result with the default heartbeat; p95 time to get there: 58 s (51 s to 58 s). The client library noticed the dead sockets through its heartbeat (25 s by default [18]). Postgres was not the bottleneck: at most 23 client sessions out of 100, 1 active. Across the 3 default-heartbeat runs, 545 of 600 clients first caught up through the state fetch after rejoining. A 5 s client heartbeat instead of 25 s (3 runs, without the 2 real browsers) did not help: 46 to 47 of 200 within 30 s, because the clients detected the cut early and tried to reconnect while the link was still down (600 attempts refused per run), then came back on the library's retry schedule [18] into the same congested gateway. For contrast, when every venue connection was reset at once with no silent period (deliberately severe), 198/200 were current within 30 s, p95 3.7 s (3.1 s to 6.2 s), and every round opened and closed with every decision acknowledged. That is consistent with a reset freeing the gateway's old connections where a silent cut leaves them open, but the comparison is not isolated: the reset runs also used the seeded limits, no real browsers, no 20 s silence, and time is counted from the reset rather than from the link's return.
4. **Headroom above 200.** With Realtime limits raised, the first step, 300 devices, failed: submit p95 never, worst round 0/302 screens current within 5 s, local gateway logged "worker_connections are not enough" 805 times; the facilitator's open requests failed for both rounds. The generator was not saturated (scheduling lateness p99 3 ms, CPU max 7 %). On this machine the ceiling above 200 is the local gateway's connection limit, a property of the CLI stack and not a hosted Supabase figure; no breakpoint of the app, Realtime or Postgres was observed up to 200 devices.
5. **Everyone refreshing at once.** In the targeted refresh phase of the 20 min soak, 36/200 simultaneous token refreshes succeeded. Each was tried once with no retry, unlike supabase-js, which retries a refresh that fails on the network. The others failed with connection resets (UND_ERR_SOCKET x93, ECONNRESET x71); the local gateway logged `worker_connections are not enough` 1 time in that run, so it is the likely cause, not a demonstrated one. The soak itself held: 26 rounds, worst round 202/202 screens current within 5 s, no acknowledged decision lost.

Across all 30 runs: 0 acknowledged decisions lost; 299 decisions never acknowledged, all in 6 runs where the local gateway was saturated (recovery-r1, recovery-r2, recovery-heartbeat-5s-r1, recovery-heartbeat-5s-r2, recovery-heartbeat-5s-r3, headroom-300-r1); 160 refused because their round was never opened; 0 runs with a score that did not match the stored decisions; 443 retried submissions answered as duplicates and stored once. Broadcast from the database delivered the final result with a worst-round p95 of 27 ms (19 ms to 34 ms) against 417 ms (342 ms to 531 ms) for Postgres Changes, to the same 202 measured screens with the same payload and authorization (the Postgres Changes reference also carried 2 real browsers, the Broadcast runs 0). Publishing after every decision met the 5 s target in 3 of 3 runs with raised limits; with the seeded limits (1 run), 200/202 screens got there, because of the connection quota. No message-rate limit was logged in any configuration tried; the per-second figure is averaged by the server [16], so this does not predict a hosted project's behaviour.
